1The goal

Be the first to control 5 connected provinces (8 on the big board).

Rivers split the board into provinces: blocks of 2×2 squares (9 provinces on the 6×6 board, 16 on the 8×8 board). You control a province when only you have soldiers in it. If both colours are inside, nobody controls it.

The provinces you control must be connected. Every province is separated from the next by a river, and a river can only be crossed on a bridge, so your provinces are joined by your bridges. Loose provinces do not count.

The win is checked at the end of every turn: whoever has the connected provinces at the end of their own turn wins.

3Your turn: roll, re-roll, pass

  1. Roll the dice. The two numbers light up two squares, on opposite sides of the board. You will play on one of them.
  2. Re-roll if you want. Once per turn you may throw the dice again. The new roll replaces the old one, so you cannot go back to the first squares.
  3. Make one move on a lit square (sections 5 to 10).
  4. The turn passes to your opponent.

No legal move? If the lit squares give you nothing to do, tap Re-roll. If the new roll gives nothing either, tap Pass and the turn goes to your opponent. The Pass button appears only after you have used your re-roll.

One turn is one move: one action, on one lit square. You never get two actions in a turn.

4The lit squares: what can I do?

What you may do depends on where the lit square is. Look at it first.

Where the lit square isWhat you may do
Inside your territory: your own base, an empty square of a province you control, or any square joined to them by your bridges Land on it (it is yours or empty).
Move or attack from any base of that territory.
Withdraw from any base of that territory.
Transport from any base of that territory.
Build a bridge from any base of that territory.
Outside your territory, but touching one of your bases Land on it, only if it is empty and not enemy territory.
Move or attack onto it, from a base that touches it.
Build a bridge onto it, from a base that touches it.
No withdraw, no transport.
Anywhere else (not yours, and none of your bases touches it)Land on it, only if it is empty and not enemy territory. Nothing else.
An enemy base, or an empty square inside enemy territory, and none of your bases touches itNothing. If both lit squares are like this you have no legal move: re-roll.

The one rule people forget: new soldiers (landing and reinforcing) go only on the lit square. Having a big territory does not let you reinforce any base you like. Section 5 shows exactly what is allowed.

"Your territory" is explained in section 12. In short: your bases plus the squares of provinces you control, joined by your bridges.

5Land: new soldiers

Bring soldiers from your camp onto the board. Landing on one of your own bases is also how you reinforce it.

When
The lit square is empty (and not enemy territory), or it is one of your bases.
Where
Only on the lit square. Never on another base of yours, never next to it.
How many
Any number from 1 up to everything in your camp.
Result
The soldiers stand on the lit square. If it was empty, a new base appears.

6Move

Take soldiers from one of your bases and put them on a square next to it.

When
Any lit square from which you can reach a start base (section 4): a lit square in your territory, or a lit square touching one of your bases.
From
Lit square in your territory: any base of that territory (and any base touching the lit square).
Lit square outside your territory: only the bases that touch it.
To
A square that shares a side with the start base (up, down, left or right). From a base of your territory this can be any neighbouring square. From a base outside your territory it can only be the lit square.
How many
Any number from 1 up to all the soldiers of that base. Moving all of them leaves the square empty.
Rivers
Across a river you need a bridge. One of yours is free to use. With no bridge, the move builds yours (it uses one from your supply). You cannot use an enemy bridge.
Result
The soldiers join the target square (an empty square, or one of your bases). If it holds enemy soldiers, the move is an attack (section 7).

Allowed

Not allowed

7Attack

An attack is a move onto a square that holds enemy soldiers. It follows every rule of Move (section 6): same start bases, same neighbouring squares, same river rules.

How it ends
Each attacker kills one defender.
Defenders
The ones that are killed are gone for good.
Attackers
All the attackers leave their base. Those that killed are worn out and go back to your camp. Attackers beyond the number of defenders stay on the captured square.
Attackers vs defendersResult
5 vs 33 defenders die. 2 attackers stay and the square is yours (3 return to camp).
3 vs 3All 3 defenders die. The square is left empty. Your 3 attackers return to camp.
2 vs 52 defenders die, 3 defenders remain. Your 2 attackers return to camp.

Across a river the attack also uses a bridge, exactly like a move: one of yours is free, with no bridge it builds yours.

9Transport

Send soldiers from one of your bases far along a straight line, inside your territory, in a single move.

When
Only when the lit square is inside your territory.
From
Any base of that territory.
The line
Straight along a row or a column, any distance. It may only pass through your own territory: your bases and the empty squares of provinces you control. An enemy base or a square outside your territory stops it. A river can be crossed only on your own bridge.
To
Any square of your territory along the line: one of your bases, or an empty square of a province you control (a new base appears).
How many
Any number from 1 up to all the soldiers of the start base. They all go to the same destination: no splitting.

Allowed

Not allowed

10Build a bridge

Place one of your bridges across a river. You can do it on its own, or together with a move: building a bridge and moving soldiers over it is one move, never two turns.

When
Like Move: the lit square is in your territory, or touches one of your bases.
Where
Between one of your bases and the square across the river from it (the square can be empty, yours, or the enemy's).
Cost
One bridge from your supply, for good.
Soldiers
None (a bridge only), or any number from 1 up to all of them (a move or attack across the new bridge).
Replace
If an enemy bridge already connects the two squares and you hold both ends with your soldiers, you may replace it with yours. The enemy bridge disappears.

Allowed

Not allowed

11Rivers and bridges: the rules

  • A square next to a river is separated from the square across it. To go across (move, attack, bridge, transport) you need a bridge.
  • A bridge belongs to the player who built it. Only its owner can cross it.
  • Your bridge: you cross it freely and for free.
  • No bridge: a move or attack across the river builds yours (it uses one from your supply). Transport cannot build one.
  • Enemy bridge: you cannot cross it. The only thing you can do is replace it, when you hold both ends (section 10).
  • Out of bridges: with none left in your supply you cannot cross a river unless a bridge of yours is already there.
  • There is at most one bridge between two squares.

Why bridges matter: your provinces are connected only through your bridges. A bridge is what turns a few provinces into a winning chain, and replacing the enemy's bridge can cut his chain.

12Provinces and territory

Controlling a province

A province is yours when the soldiers inside it are all yours. It is the enemy's when they are all his. If both colours have soldiers in it, nobody controls it. An empty province belongs to nobody.

Your territory

Your territory is made of:

  • all your bases, and
  • all the squares of every province you control, even the empty ones,

joined together when squares touch, or when they are on two sides of a river that has your bridge. Two pieces separated by a river with no bridge of yours are two separate territories.

What the territory does for you

  • A lit square in your territory lets you move, attack, withdraw, transport and build bridges from any base of it.
  • Transport runs through it.
  • In a province both of you are in, the squares are not territory for either of you. That is why you may land there, but cannot transport through it.
  • You can never land in enemy territory.

13Winning, and common questions

You win at the end of your own turn if you control 5 provinces (8 on the big board) that are connected in one territory.

QuestionAnswer
Can I reinforce any base in my territory?No. New soldiers go only on the lit square (section 5).
Can I play on both lit squares?No. One move per turn, on one of the two squares.
Can I land next to enemy soldiers?Yes, if the lit square is empty and not inside enemy territory (a province that holds both colours is fine).
Can I move soldiers from a base that is not on the lit square?Yes, if the lit square is in your territory: any base of it (section 4).
Do I have to move all the soldiers of a base?No. Any number from 1 to all of them.
What happens to a base when all its soldiers leave?It disappears: the square becomes empty.
Can I skip my turn?Only with Pass, which appears after you have used your re-roll.
Do attackers die?No. They are worn out and go back to your camp. Only defenders die for good.
I have no bridges left. Am I stuck?Not completely: you can still cross on bridges of yours that are already built, and play inside a river-bounded area.
Can the enemy cross my bridge?No. Only the owner can. He can only replace it if he holds both ends.
