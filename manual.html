Province

A strategy game of bridges and borders

How to play2 players6×6 and 8×8 boardsfree, in your browser

province-game.com

1The goal

Be the first to control 5 connected provinces (8 on the big board).

Rivers split the board into provinces, blocks of 2×2 squares (9 provinces on the 6×6 board, 16 on the 8×8 board). You control a province when only you have soldiers in it. If both colours are inside, nobody controls it.

The provinces you control must be connected: the only way across a river is a bridge, so your provinces are joined by your bridges. Loose provinces do not count.

The win is checked at the end of every turn. Whoever has the connected provinces at the end of their turn wins.

Soldiers that are not on the board wait in your camp. Soldiers that die are gone for good (the graveyard).

Bridges are limited. You get a fixed number for the whole game, and every bridge you build is gone for good. The counter next to the arch icon shows how many you have left.

3Your turn

  1. Roll the dice. The two numbers light up two squares, on opposite sides of the board.
  2. Re-roll if you want. Once per turn you may throw the dice again. The new roll replaces the old one, so you cannot go back to the first squares.
  3. Make one move on a lit square (see section 4).
  4. The turn passes to your opponent.

No legal move? If the lit squares give you nothing to do, tap Re-roll. If the new roll gives nothing either, you tap Pass and the turn goes to your opponent. Pass only appears after you have used your re-roll.

4What a lit square lets you do

⬇
LandNew soldiers from your camp come down on a lit square that is empty or already yours. They land only on the lit square, never in enemy territory.
➜
MoveTake any number of soldiers from a base to a square next to it. Moving across a river builds a bridge (see section 5).
⚔
AttackMove onto a square held by the enemy. Each attacker kills one defender (see below).
⬆
WithdrawPull soldiers from a base back to your camp.
⇢
TransportSend soldiers from a base in a straight line through your own territory to another of your bases, in one go.
⌒
Build a bridgePlace a bridge across a river. You can also move soldiers over it in the same move: building a bridge and moving is one turn, not two.

Which squares can you use?

  • A lit square inside your territory lets you play from any of your bases there: it does not have to be the base you move from.
  • A lit square that is next to one of your bases lets you move or attack onto it from that base.
  • A lit square deep in enemy territory with none of your bases next to it: you cannot use it.

Territory

Your territory is the connected area made of your bases and your controlled provinces (even their empty squares), joined by your bridges. The bigger it is, the more lit squares you can use.

5Attacks, rivers and bridges

Attack

  • Each attacking soldier kills one defender.
  • The defenders that die are gone for good.
  • The attackers that did the killing are worn out: they leave the board and go back to your camp to rest.
  • Extra attackers (more than defenders) stay on the captured square. Equal numbers leave the square empty.

Rivers and bridges

  • You can only cross a river on a bridge. Moving across a river with no bridge builds yours, as part of the move.
  • A bridge belongs to the player who built it. Only they can cross it.
  • You can replace an enemy bridge with yours if you hold the squares at both ends.
